Texas's 2026 Reload: How the Georgia Loss Lit the Fuse

Sarkisian ran a full roster and scheme overhaul this offseason — Muschamp, the trenches, the portal, and Arch.

Georgia has been the wall. Kirby Smart's Bulldogs went 2–0 against Texas in 2024 and beat them again 35–10 in November 2025. Rather than tweak, head coach Steve Sarkisian ran a full schematic and roster overhaul across the 2026 offseason — targeting the trenches, the coaching staff, and physical depth to close the gap.

1. The Coaching Counter: Hiring Will Muschamp

The most direct shot at Smart was hiring SEC veteran Will Muschamp as defensive coordinator, replacing Pete Kwiatkoski.

  • Muschamp was a key architect of Georgia's back-to-back national championship defenses.
  • He knows Smart's schematic vulnerabilities, tendencies, and building blocks firsthand.
  • His aggressive, turn-them-loose style is meant to replicate the physical presence Georgia used to overpower Texas.

2. Matching Georgia's Trench Brutality

Georgia has repeatedly won by dominating the line of scrimmage — choking the run game and pressuring the quarterback. Texas answered by reloading both lines with size.

  • Offensive line: returning starters Trevor Goosby and Connor Robertson, plus portal depth in Melvin Siani and Laurence Seymore to hold up in the 4th quarter.
  • Defensive interior: around edge rusher Colin Simmons, heavy interior rotation via Arkansas transfer Ian Geffrard and LSU transfer Zion Williams.

3. Portal Aggression to Erase the Blue-Chip Gap

Georgia historically carried more four- and five-star talent down the depth chart. Sarkisian used the transfer portal as an immediate equalizer instead of waiting on high school development.

  • One of the program's most dominant transfer classes ever for 2026.
  • SEC-ready athletes prioritized to fill holes — including Pitt linebacker Rasheem Biles as a physical middle-field enforcer.

4. Fully Unleashing Arch Manning

The offense now centers entirely on Arch Manning as the definitive QB1. Sarkisian's playbook leans on faster RPO sequences and horizontal spreading — built to neutralize heavy pass rushes and keep Georgia's linebackers out of the backfield.

The shift has moved national perception. Heading into 2026, analysts including Paul Finebaum have picked Texas to finally dethrone Georgia and win the SEC Championship.
